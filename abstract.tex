\noindent Neutron stars are compact objects whose hydrostatic equilibrium is determined by the equation of state (EOS) of the dense matter in their interiors, which makes them natural laboratories for nuclear physics at densities inaccessible to terrestrial experiments.
When two neutron stars merge, part of their mass is ejected and, as demonstrated by the gravitational wave event GW170817 and its associated kilonova, this ejected material is a site of rapid neutron capture (r-process) nucleosynthesis.
The yield of heavy nuclei in the ejecta depends on the properties of neutron rich nuclei far from stability, including nuclear masses, decay rates, neutron capture rates, fission properties among others much of them unknown.
Since nuclear physics play a crucial rol on the volution of observables, understanding the impact of relying on theoretical nuclear physics results is essential to correctly interpret current and future multimessenger observations.

This work presents a detail analysis of the impact of different theoretical $\alpha-$ and $\beta^{-}-$decay rates in the kilonova evolution.
The nucleosynthesis was calculated using the nuclear reaction network WinNet across an ensemble of different initial thermodynamic conditions parametrized by initial electron fraction ($Y_{e,0}$), expansion timescale ($\tau$) and initial entropy per baryon ($s_{0}$).
The kilonova light curve were calculated base on current semianalytical models based on radiative transfer simulations. 
The results shows that the heating rate from radioactive decays deviate from the widely accepted power law behavior depending on the $\beta^{-}-$decay prescription even for different initial conditions. 
In addition the set of nuclei responsible for such behavior was identified and the description of their decay chains are presented.

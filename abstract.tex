\noindent Neutron stars are compact objects whose hydrostatic equilibrium is determined by the equation of state (EOS) of the dense matter in their interiors, which makes them natural laboratories for nuclear physics at densities inaccessible to terrestrial experiments.
Although measurements of neutron-star masses, radii, and tidal deformabilities have constrained the pressure of dense matter, there is still no consensus on the composition of the stellar core, in particular on whether a phase transition to deconfined quark matter takes place.
Binary neutron-star mergers (BNSMs) probe even higher densities and temperatures and, as demonstrated by the gravitational-wave event GW170817 and its associated kilonova, are a site of rapid neutron-capture (r-process) nucleosynthesis.
In these events, the EOS shapes the hydrodynamic evolution of the merger and may therefore leave fingerprints both in the gravitational waveform and in the mass and initial thermodynamic conditions (e.g., density and entropy) of the ejected material.
The subsequent nucleosynthesis in the ejecta, however, also depends on the properties of neutron-rich nuclei far from stability, including nuclear masses, $\beta^{-}$-decay and neutron-capture rates, and fission properties.
Because dense-matter and nuclear-physics uncertainties affect the same observables, disentangling them is essential to determine what current and future multimessenger observations can reveal about dense matter.
In this work, we perform a joint analysis of the gravitational-wave and electromagnetic signatures of BNSMs to investigate whether a hadron--quark phase transition leaves an imprint on the gravitational-wave signal and on the ejected material, whose mass and composition determine the radioactive heating that powers the kilonova.
To this end, we perform general-relativistic hydrodynamic simulations of an equal-mass BNSM with the Einstein Toolkit, using a hadronic EOS, a hybrid EOS that coincides with it below the onset of a first-order hadron--quark phase transition, and an alternative hadronic EOS.
Since the inspiral is identical for the hadronic and hybrid models by construction, post-merger differences can be attributed to the phase transition, while the alternative hadronic EOS allows us to assess whether the combined gravitational-wave and electromagnetic signatures can distinguish a phase transition from a change in the stiffness of hadronic matter.
We compare the post-merger gravitational-wave signals and the dynamical ejecta, whose thermodynamic evolution, neglecting neutrino interactions, we post-process with the nuclear reaction network WinNet to obtain r-process abundances and radioactive heating rates, from which we compute kilonova light curves.
Finally, we quantify the impact of the phase transition relative to the variation of the hadronic EOS, to uncertainties in nuclear-physics inputs such as fission-fragment distributions and $\beta^{-}$-decay rates, and to numerical resolution.
